# Body composition measurement: InBody reliability, tracking change, other tools, and a protocol

Last updated: 2026-11-06

Scope: how much trust to put in a gym InBody scan when tracking a recomposition. Four questions: how repeatable multi-frequency segmental bioimpedance (MF-BIA) is and what moves it (KQ1); whether it tracks real change in fat and lean mass against DXA or multi-compartment models (KQ2); what the other tools offer (DXA, waist, skinfolds, photos, daily bodyweight; KQ3); and a measurement protocol for this lifter (KQ4, inference). Method: PubMed searches and web searches for each question, then the primary source. Each finding says whether the full text, the abstract, or a secondary summary was read. Numbers not found in a primary source are listed under Gaps. Units: findings keep the source's units (mostly kg); the applied parts add pounds (1 kg = 2.205 lb). Tags: **[Evidence]** peer-reviewed; **[Practitioner]** coaches, apps, practitioner sites; **[Convention]** manufacturer, vendor or protocol documents. Pre-2016 work is marked foundational.

## KQ1. How repeatable is an InBody scan, and what moves it?

### Takeaway

Run back to back, an InBody 770 barely varies (TEM 0.14% body fat). Across days, under lab standardization, the error is about 1.2 percentage points of body fat and 0.75-0.9 kg of fat or fat-free mass. That puts the 95% minimal detectable change (MDC95) at about 2-3.3 points of body fat and about 1.5-2.4 kg (3.3-5.4 lb) of fat mass or fat-free mass. Most of the day-to-day error is biological: water, food, posture and recent exercise. A glass of water, a meal the day before, or an upper-body workout can each move fat or fat-free mass by about 1-2 kg. That is the size of the change being tracked.

**Manufacturer guidance and InBody outputs**

- **[Convention, manufacturer] InBody USA test guidelines** ([inbodyusa.com](https://inbodyusa.com/general/inbody-test/)), read in full:
  - Do: normal fluid intake the day before and **"hydrate one hour prior to testing"**; stand upright 5-10 min first; test at the same time of day every time; use the restroom first; allow 20 min after hot or cold exposure.
  - Do not: exercise beforehand ("test only after fully recovered (1-3 days)"); eat within 3 h; wear socks or heavy clothing; have lotion on hands or feet.
  - Low-carb or keto diets "may skew fat mass/PBF results".
  - "We recommend testing every 2-4 weeks under the same conditions."
  - The page does not mention alcohol. Gym and clinic handouts commonly add no alcohol for 24 h before. Those handouts are secondary.
- **[Convention, manufacturer]** InBody 770: six frequencies (1, 5, 50, 250, 500, 1000 kHz). It reports impedance at all six, reactance at 5, 50 and 250 kHz, and phase angle at 50 kHz. LBM is estimated from TBW by a proprietary algorithm (Tinsley 2022a, from manufacturer-supplied text). InBody 570: three frequencies (5, 50, 500 kHz) (Nickerson & Snarr 2022, full text).
- **[Convention, manufacturer]** InBody result-sheet guide ([au.inbody.com](https://au.inbody.com/result-sheet-interpretation/)), read in full:
  - ECW/TBW: "acceptable range being between 0.360 – 0.390", average about 0.380. Above 0.390 "may indicate swelling or excess ECW". High FFM with a high ratio usually means extra water, not muscle.
  - Segmental lean values reflect fat-free mass, not muscle alone.
  - The page gives no meaning for values below 0.360.

### Inferences

- **MDC for this lifter's device (InBody 770 lab figures as a proxy).** MDC95 = 1.96 × √2 × TEM = 2.77 × TEM.
  - With Velasquez's between-day TEM: body fat **3.3 points**; FM and FFM **2.1-2.4 kg (4.6-5.4 lb)**.
  - McLester's "minimum difference" gives a similar range: 2.1-2.7 points; 1.5-2.4 kg (3.3-5.3 lb).
  - These are lab numbers under a strict overnight dry fast. A gym scan with looser control will be noisier.
- **The InBody 570 has no test-retest study.** McLester found the 230, 720 and 770 similar, so the 770 numbers are used for the 570 too. That assumption is untested.
- **SMM has no published MDC.** Assume SMM error scales with FFM. The lifter's SMM is 58% of his FFM (39.9 of 68.5 kg), giving an SMM MDC95 of about 1.2-1.4 kg (2.7-3.1 lb). Tinsley 2022a shows SMM moving with LBM when water shifts, so this is a lower bound.
- **Standardization matters more than the device.** Technical error is 0.14% body fat; between-day error is about 1.2%. Day-to-day biology, not the machine, dominates. Every factor in the table above is controllable.
- **ECW/TBW 0.354** sits below InBody's 0.360-0.390 band. InBody attaches no warning to low values, so it is not a flag. Use it as a hydration-consistency check: if FFM jumps and the ratio moves a lot between scans, suspect water. A ratio that stays above 0.390 together with visible swelling is a reason to see a professional, not something to train around.

### Gaps

- No test-retest data for the InBody 570, and none for InBody SMM.
- Velasquez 2026's per-device tables (InBody within-day values, ICCs) were images and were not read. McLester 2020's men-only values for the 770 are behind a paywall.
- Device names in Tinsley 2017 and Rodriguez 2023 were not in the abstracts.
- No study of alcohol on BIA readings. No study of a 10-mile race on next-day or 3-day-later BIA.

## KQ2. Does an InBody track change in fat and lean mass?

### Takeaway

At the **group** level, the InBody 770 tracks change in fat mass and fat-free mass about as well as DXA over 4-10 weeks in trained young men. For **one person**, the error of a change score against DXA or a four-compartment (4C) model is about 1.0-1.5 kg for FM and FFM and 1.2-2.6 points for body fat. That is as large as a 12-week recomposition. Fluid shifts register as lean mass: creatine loading, glycogen loading and food intake all raise InBody FFM by 1-2 kg with little new muscle. The absolute value is offset too. Against DXA, InBody reads about 3-4 points lower body fat in men and 1.5-3.3 kg more FFM. Against 4C, the offset can point the other way.

### Inferences

- **What two scans can show.** The plan expects −5 to −9 lb fat (−2.3 to −4.1 kg) and +2 to +5 lb lean (+0.9 to +2.3 kg) in 12 weeks.
  - Fat: only the upper half of that range clears the FM MDC95 of about 5 lb.
  - Lean: most of the range sits below the FFM MDC95.
  - So two InBody scans can plausibly confirm the fat loss but cannot confirm the lean gain.
  - The expected fall in PBF (16.5% to 13-14%, 2.5-3.5 points) is borderline against an MDC95 of about 3 points.
- **Direction of error in a recomposition.** Tinsley & Moore 2020 found MF-BIA did worse than DXA exactly when FFM rose and FM fell. Treat a large InBody "lean gain" as possibly partly water until the scale, waist and strength trends agree.
- **Creatine.** At 3-5 g/day, saturation takes about 4 weeks (Hultman 1996), so water gain is slower and probably smaller than after loading. The loading studies put the upper bound at about 1-2 kg of TBW, body mass or InBody FFM. If creatine started less than about 4 weeks before the 21 Oct baseline, part of any FFM gain at the next scan will be creatine water. The exact start date was not recorded.
- **Carbohydrate and glycogen.** A carbohydrate-heavy day or two before a scan, or a race carb-load, can add about 1-2 kg of "lean" (DXA data; BIA is likely similar or worse given Tinsley 2017). A depleting long run can subtract some. Matching the 48-72 h before each scan matters as much as the morning itself.
- **Absolute value.** The 16.5% InBody reading likely corresponds to about 19.5-21% on DXA (Potter, Nickerson: about −3 to −4 points in men). The 4C truth may sit between the two. Compare InBody only with the same InBody. Do not compare it with DXA norms or a DXA scan without allowing for that offset (`research/body-recomposition.md` KQ3 makes the same point).

### Gaps

- No study tests InBody change scores in lean (<15% body fat) trained men during a deficit with a 4C criterion. The closest are Antonio 2019 (mixed sex, DXA criterion, 4 weeks) and Tinsley 2022b (surplus, not deficit).
- Schoenfeld 2020's device model, and full-text limits of agreement for change, were not verified.
- No study measures water gain on InBody at maintenance doses of creatine (3-5 g/day) rather than loading.

## KQ3. Other tools: DXA, waist, skinfolds, photos and daily bodyweight

### Takeaway

DXA is about three times more precise than InBody between days (TEM <0.4% body fat, <0.3 kg). Its LSC is about 0.8-1.4 kg for lean or fat mass, but it is also moved by glycogen and water. A commercial scan costs about $40-125 in the US. Waist circumference is cheap and repeatable if one site and one method are used every time. The two standard sites read nearly the same in men. Skinfolds are least affected by day-to-day water but need a trained measurer; report them as a sum in mm, not as %BF. Daily bodyweight scatters about 0.5% (about 0.9 lb) around its trend, and errors on neighbouring days are correlated. A 0.5 lb/week trend takes about 4 weeks of daily weigh-ins, or 4-5 weeks at 3 per week, to separate from noise.

Men's TEM: body mass 0.29 kg; fat 0.52 kg and 0.66%; FFM 0.42 kg. Segmental values were worse (fat 0.72-1.94% in men). The interval between scans was not stated in the abstract. Abstract.
- **[Evidence] Velasquez 2026** (KQ1): iDXA had the best between-day reliability (TEM <0.4% body fat, <0.3 kg FM and FFM).
- **[Evidence] Nana et al. 2016**, *Int J Sport Nutr Exerc Metab* 26(3):259-267 (epub 2014), [PubMed 24458265](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24458265/). 21 male cyclists. Scans taken after random daily activity had change-score SDs of 2-3% for total and fat-free soft tissue mass, about double those of fasted, rested scans (1-2%). Abstract.
- **[Evidence, foundational, pre-2016] Nana et al. 2015**, *Int J Sport Nutr Exerc Metab* 25(2):198-215, [PubMed 25029265](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25029265/). Best practice: rested, overnight-fasted, minimal clothing, standard positioning, consistent regions of interest. Abstract.
- **[Convention] ISCD official positions on DXA body composition**, Hangartner et al. 2013, *J Clin Densitom* 16(4):520-536, [PubMed 24183641](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24183641/). Covers precision assessment and repeatability. Abstract only; the specific positions were not read. LSC = 2.77 × precision error is the formula Siedler 2023 used.
- DXA is not immune to water and glycogen: Toomey 2017, Bone 2017 and Tinsley 2017 (KQ1 and KQ2).
- **Availability.** **[Convention, vendor]** BodySpec's 2026 price guide claims $45 per scan, or $40 with a membership ([BodySpec](https://www.bodyspec.com/blog/post/body_scan_near_me_pricing_comparison_guide_2026)). Third-party guides quote $75-250, median about $125 (search summary only). **[Convention]** Health Physics Society: a whole-body DXA dose is about 3 µSv, about 9 hours of US natural background ([HPS](https://hps.org/publicinformation/ate/q13693/)). Search summary only.

**Waist circumference**

- **[Evidence] Ross et al. 2020**, *Nat Rev Endocrinol* 16(3):177-189, IAS/ICCR consensus, [PMC7027970](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7027970/). Read in part (first 100,000 characters) via a tool summary.
  - WHO site: "the midpoint between the lower border of the rib cage and the iliac crest". NIH site: "the superior border of the iliac crest". Either is acceptable; "no consensus exists on the optimal protocol".
  - In men the two sites give nearly identical values; in women they differ by about 2 cm.
  - Self-measurements correlate 0.8-0.9 with technician measurements but run about 1-3 cm low.
  - The consensus gives no threshold for a meaningful change.
- **[Evidence, foundational, pre-2016] Wang et al. 2003**, *Am J Clin Nutr* 77(2):379-384, [PubMed 12540397](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12540397/). Four sites (below the lowest rib, narrowest waist, midpoint, above the iliac crest). One experienced observer, triplicate measurements in 93 people: ICC >0.99 at all four sites. The sites differ in absolute value. Abstract.
- **[Evidence, foundational, pre-2016] Verweij et al. 2013**, *Public Health Nutr* 16(2):281-288, [PMC10271771](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10271771/). Literature review: measurement error ranged from 0.7 to 15 cm. The authors proposed a clinically relevant change of 3.0-6.8 cm short term and 1.8-4.1 cm maintained. Recommends one protocol, training, and repeated measurements. Abstract.
- **[Evidence] Barrios et al. 2016**, *BMC Med Res Methodol* 16:49, [PMC4855335](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4855335/). 41 mothers trained by a short video. TEM of duplicate self- and technician measurements 0.08-0.76 in. Waist ICC self vs technician 0.97; home vs lab ICC ≥0.87. Abstract.
- **[Convention]** Protocol documents citing the 2008 WHO expert consultation describe a stretch-resistant tape at constant 100 g tension, measured at the end of a normal expiration, arms relaxed at the sides. Secondary; the WHO report PDF could not be retrieved.

**Skinfolds**

- **[Evidence, narrative review] Kasper et al. 2021**, *Nutrients* 13(4):1075, [PMC8065383](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8065383/). Read in part via a tool summary.
  - Skinfolds are "the least affected method by everyday activities, ingestion of a meal and changes in hydration status". The claim is qualitative; no effect sizes are given.
  - Report the sum of the 8 ISAK sites in mm; converting to %BF "should be discouraged". In one player, body fat ranged from 4 to 8% depending on the equation.
  - A 1 cm error in site location can change the result meaningfully.
- **[Evidence] Aandstad 2014** (KQ1): in men, skinfolds, or skinfolds combined with SF-BIA, were the most valid against DXA. The best methods had validity limits of about ±3.5 points.

**Photos and smartphone imaging**

- No peer-reviewed validation of plain progress photos was found. Standardized photos (same light, distance, pose, time of day) are a **[Convention]**.
- **[Evidence] Graybeal, Brandner & Tinsley 2022**, *Clin Nutr* 41(11):2464-2472, [PubMed 36215866](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36215866/). 184 adults; two AI photo apps (Amazon Halo, myBVI) vs 4C.
  - Precision: ICC ≥0.99; precision error 0.3-0.6% for %fat and 0.2-0.5 kg for FM and FFM.
  - Validity: RMSE 3.9-6.2% for %fat and 3.1-5.2 kg for FM and FFM. Only Halo's %fat was equivalent to 4C.
  - Amazon discontinued Halo in 2023 (general knowledge, not re-checked this round).
  - Abstract.
- **[Evidence] Graybeal et al. 2023**, *J Hum Nutr Diet* 36(3):905-911, [PMC10198803](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10198803/). An imaging app trained on a 4C model, 102 adults: ICC 0.96-0.99, RMS-CV 0.5-3.0%, equivalent to 4C at the group level, with proportional bias at higher fat levels. App not named in the abstract. Abstract.

**Daily bodyweight: noise and smoothing**

- **[Evidence, foundational, pre-2016] Khosla & Billewicz 1964**, *Br J Nutr* 18:227-239, [doi:10.1079/BJN19640022](https://doi.org/10.1079/BJN19640022). Full text read (PDF). 19 people from infants to adults, weighed daily for 30-40 days.
  - SD of weight about its trend was close to **0.5% of bodyweight**.
  - Day-to-day change rarely exceeded **1.5%**.
  - Deviations from trend were positively correlated on neighbouring days: **r = 0.37 at 1 day, 0.14 at 2 days, 0.03 at 3 days**.
  - Evening weights were higher and more variable than morning weights.
  - Earlier work it cites: one man with SD 0.51% and day-to-day changes up to 1 kg (Elkinton & Danowski 1955); soldiers with daily changes above 0.5 kg on more than 100 occasions (Edholm 1961).
- **[Evidence] Turicchi et al. 2020a**, *JMIR Mhealth Uhealth* 8(9):e17977, [PMC7519428](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7519428/). Full text. 50 smart-scale users (70% women, age 49, BMI 29.3, about 336 weigh-ins each). Kalman smoothing or an exponentially weighted moving average (EWMA) predicted a missing day best: RMSE **0.62-0.64%** of bodyweight. This is the gap between one day and its smoothed neighbours, so it approximates day-level noise.
- **[Evidence] Turicchi et al. 2020b**, *PLoS One* 15(4):e0232152, [PMC7192384](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7192384/). About 1,400 people in a weight-maintenance trial. Within-week fluctuation of 0.35%: gain at weekends, loss on weekdays. Christmas added 1.35% that was not fully lost in the following months. Abstract.
- **[Evidence, foundational, pre-2016] Orsama et al. 2014**, *Obes Facts* 7(1):36-47, [PMC5644907](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5644907/). 80 adults, 4,657 weigh-ins. Weight was highest on Sunday and Monday and fell through the week; the rise began on Saturday and the fall on Tuesday. Abstract.
- **[Practitioner] The Hacker's Diet** (John Walker, [fourmilab.ch](https://www.fourmilab.ch/hackdiet/e4/signalnoise.html)): trend = exponentially smoothed average with a smoothing constant of 0.9, about the lag of a 20-day moving average. Equivalent form: each day the trend moves 10% of the way toward that day's weight.
- **[Practitioner] MacroFactor** (app from the Stronger By Science team, [help page](https://help.macrofactorapp.com/dashboard/weight_trend/)): "trend weight" is a moving average weighted toward recent weigh-ins, meant to filter out water and gut content. Search summary only.

### Inferences

- **DXA LSC for this lifter (inference).** With Velasquez's iDXA between-day TEM (<0.3 kg), the LSC is below about 0.85 kg (1.9 lb). With Kutáč's men's TEM (FFM 0.42 kg, fat 0.52 kg), it is 1.2-1.4 kg (2.6-3.2 lb). Under the same control, that is roughly half to two-thirds of the InBody MDC. DXA is the better tool to confirm a 2-5 lb lean gain, but only with the same fasted, rested, glycogen-matched conditions.
- **Waist.** In men either site works; what matters is one site every time. The NIH site (top of the iliac crest) is the easier landmark to find on oneself. Take two readings and record the mean. After about four weekly sessions, compute personal TEM from the duplicates: TEM = √(Σd² / 2n). Then use 2.77 × TEM as the change threshold. Until then, assume a TEM of about 0.25 in (inside Barrios's 0.08-0.76 in) and treat changes under about 0.75 in (2 cm) as noise.
- **Skinfolds** need a trained measurer at a fixed schedule. They are optional here. If a gym coach with ISAK training is available, the sum of sites in mm is the most water-robust signal.
- **Photos** are a qualitative check only.
- **Weeks needed to see a 0.5 lb/week trend (calculation).** Model: least-squares slope through morning weights; noise SD 0.5% of 181 lb (0.91 lb, Khosla) or 0.63% (1.14 lb, Turicchi); serial correlations 0.37/0.14/0.03 (Khosla). A Monte Carlo run that added Turicchi's 0.35% weekly cycle gave the same answer.

| Weigh-ins | Weeks | 95% half-width of the slope, lb/week (SD 0.5% / 0.63%) | Reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| Daily | 2 | ±1.09 / ±1.38 | Cannot tell 0.5 lb/week from flat |
| Daily | 3 | ±0.61 / ±0.77 | Not yet |
| Daily | 4 | ±0.40 / ±0.51 | A 0.5 lb/week loss is just distinguishable from flat |
| Daily | 6 | ±0.22 / ±0.28 | Can tell 0.25 from 0.75 lb/week |
| 3 per week | 4 | ±0.49 / ±0.62 | Borderline |
| 3 per week | 6 | ±0.27 / ±0.33 | Distinguishable from flat; rough rate |
| 3 per week | 8 | ±0.17 / ±0.22 | Rate known to about ±0.2 lb/week |

- Two 7-day averages must differ by about **1.3-1.6 lb** before the difference exceeds noise at 95%. At 0.5 lb/week, that takes about 3 weeks between the two weeks compared.
- Real life is noisier than this model. Creatine water, carbohydrate swings between week types, weekend alcohol, races and long runs add step changes that a smooth noise model does not include. The 3-4-week judgement window in `data/body-composition.md` section 6 is the minimum, not a comfortable margin.
- **Irregular weigh-ins bias weekly averages.** Weight runs high on Sunday and Monday (Orsama; Turicchi 0.35%, about 0.6 lb). A week with weigh-ins only on Monday and Saturday reads higher than a week with Wednesday and Thursday. An EWMA or a slope over all weigh-ins handles gaps better than a mean of whatever days were logged (Turicchi 2020a found EWMA among the best methods at filling missing days).

### Gaps

- ISAK skinfold TEM targets (commonly quoted as 5% intra-tester for skinfolds and 1% for girths) were not verified in a primary source.
- No modern day-to-day bodyweight SD for young, lean, training men was found. Khosla's subjects were sedentary indoor workers and families; the authors expected active people to fluctuate more.
- No study links waist change to a fat-mass change in kg for lean men.
- Whether a DXA provider is available near the lifter, and its price, is unknown.

## KQ4. Applied: a measurement protocol for this lifter

All of this section is **inference** from KQ1-KQ3, and it targets `data/body-composition.md` sections 6-8 and `guide/06-recovery-nutrition-cardio.md` section 4.3.

### Takeaway

Judge the recomposition on four signals together: the scale trend over 4 or more weeks, weekly waist, main-lift strength, and InBody scans taken under identical conditions every 4-8 weeks. Count a change between two single scans as real only if it is at least about **3 points of body fat, 5 lb of fat mass, 5 lb of fat-free mass, or 3 lb of SMM**. Smaller changes count only when the other three signals point the same way, or when three or more scans form a trend.

### Inferences

**What to measure and how often**

| Signal | How | How often | 3-day week | 5-6-day week |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bodyweight | On waking, after voiding, before food or drink, same scale, minimal clothing. Log in lb | Daily if possible; at minimum 4 mornings per week including Tuesday-Friday | Same | Same. Carbohydrate is higher (about 325 vs 290 g/day) and training heavier, so expect some glycogen water; judge the trend across both week types, not week against week |
| Trend | EWMA (constant 0.9) or a 4-week slope. Section 6 rules apply to the trend, not to single days | Read weekly; decide on 4 or more weeks (daily) or 5-6 weeks (irregular) | Same | Same |
| Waist | NIH site (top of the iliac crest), tape horizontal and snug, end of a normal breath out, two readings, record the mean | Weekly, same weekday morning, after the weigh-in | Same | Same |
| Strength | e1RM on the main lifts from `data/track-log.md` | Every session (already logged) | Compare like with like | Fatigue is higher; compare across matched week types or use the tracks' trend |
| InBody | Conditions below | Every 4-8 weeks, read as a series. Minimum: 8 and 12 weeks | Scan at the end of a 3-day week or a deload, after 2 or more rest days | Do not scan during a 5-6-day week: there is no 48-h gap from training |

**Scan conditions (replicate every time)**

1. Same InBody unit. Record the model (on the printout) and location. Different analyzers are not interchangeable (Stratton 2021).
2. Same weekday and time. Baseline was a Wednesday morning, and a midweek morning also avoids the weekend weight bump.
3. Nothing to eat or drink since the night before: no water, coffee or creatine that morning. Take the day's creatine after the scan. Research protocols use this dry fast, and 0.9 L of water read as about +1.3 kg fat on an InBody 770 (Tinsley 2022a). InBody's own page says to drink water an hour before (see Conflicts). Whichever is chosen, keep it identical every time.
4. Usual eating for the 2-3 days before, at the plan's carbohydrate level for that week type. No carb-load, no low-carb day, no unusually salty or large meal the night before (Tinsley 2017; Bone 2017; InBody's low-carb warning).
5. No lifting for 48 h or more, and no hard upper-body session for 72 h if possible (Rodriguez 2023; InBody "1-3 days"). No run longer than about 60 min, and no race, for 72 h.
6. No alcohol for 72 h. With weekend drinking, a Wednesday scan leaves about 80 h after Saturday night. No direct evidence; indirect only (Hobson 2010).
7. Void the bladder. Bare feet, no lotion, the same light clothing. No shower, sauna or cold exposure in the last 20 min.
8. Stand 5-10 min, then scan. Do not stand around for 30-60 min first; LBM drifted down about 0.8 kg over 65 min upright (Tinsley 2022a).
9. Enter the same height (71 in / 180.3 cm) and age setting.
10. Optional: scan again the next morning under the same conditions and average the two. This cuts the error by about 30% if day-level errors are independent.

| Date | Use | Notes |

- If a scan has to move, keep it on a midweek morning that meets conditions 3-6. Note any deviation in the log rather than skipping the scan.

**Minimum change between scans that counts**

InBody 770 lab figures are used as a proxy for the gym unit (Velasquez 2026; McLester 2020). Thresholds are rounded.

| Output | Single scan vs single scan, 95% (MDC95) | "Probably real" (80%) | Two-scan averages at each time point, 95% |
|---|---|---|---|
| PBF | **3 points** (2.1-3.3) | 2 points | 2.3 points |
| Fat mass | **5 lb** (3.3-5.4 lb; 1.5-2.4 kg) | 3-3.5 lb | 3.2-3.8 lb |
| FFM / LBM | **5 lb** (3.5-5.4 lb) | 3-3.5 lb | 3.2-3.8 lb |
| SMM | **3 lb** (scaled from FFM; no direct data) | 2 lb | 2 lb |

- The 80% column is 1.28 × √2 × TEM. The two-scan column divides TEM by √2. Both assume lab-level standardization; a gym scan with looser control needs more.
- Across three or more scans, fit a line through the values rather than comparing two scans.
- A rise in FFM or SMM together with an ECW/TBW shift, a weigh-in bump, or a creatine start points to water.

### Gaps

- The InBody model and the conditions of the 21 Oct baseline were not recorded. Did he drink water, eat, train the day before, or drink alcohol that weekend? If the next scan cannot match, the baseline-to-scan-2 change carries extra, unknown error. Ask the lifter.

## Conflicts

- **Direction of InBody bias.** Against DXA, InBody reads lower body fat and higher FFM in men: about −3 to −4 points and +1.6 to +3.3 kg (Potter 2022; Nickerson & Snarr 2022; McLester 2020; Antonio 2019). Against 4C, Brewer 2021 found the InBody 770 may read **higher** body fat and lower FFM. The review of 12 studies against 4C (Oliver 2026) found mean biases in both directions. Both are kept. DXA itself reads high against multi-compartment models (secondary citation in Nickerson & Snarr 2022). Practical consequence: compare InBody only with the same InBody.
- **Water before the scan.** InBody's own page says to "hydrate one hour prior to testing". Research protocols use an overnight dry fast (Velasquez 2026; Nickerson & Snarr 2022; Siedler 2023), and Tinsley 2022a showed water read as fat mass on an InBody 770. Both are recorded. This protocol picks the dry fast for its smaller, known error. Matching whatever was done at baseline matters more than which choice is made.
- **How often to scan.** InBody recommends every 2-4 weeks. The MDC suggests two scans need about 8-12 weeks of real change to exceed noise. Not a true conflict: frequent scans help if read as a series, and mislead if two adjacent scans are compared.
- **Is fasting needed?** Androutsos 2015 concluded fasting is not needed for clinical BIA (about 1 point of change). For tracking a 2-3-point change that 1 point is a third of the signal, and Tinsley 2017 found BIA did not fully reset after an overnight fast. The difference is the use case: one-off clinical measures vs serial tracking.
- **Which field method is most reliable.** Kasper 2021 calls skinfolds the least affected by day-to-day variation (qualitative). Aandstad 2014 found SF-BIA the most reliable on test-retest and skinfolds the most valid in men. Different metrics; both kept.
- **InBody vs DXA for recomposition change.** Schoenfeld 2020 and Antonio 2019 call MF-BIA "an acceptable alternative" for tracking change. Tinsley & Moore 2020 found DXA better when fat falls and lean rises, and Boykin 2021 found weak individual agreement (CCC about 0.5). Group-level agreement and individual-level error are different questions. This note uses the individual-level error.
- **Refinement of `research/body-recomposition.md` KQ2.** That note says InBody "varies by a few percentage points of body fat between sessions". The cited figures: typical between-day error about 1.2 points (TEM) under strict control; 95% threshold for a real change about 2-3.3 points. "A few points" fits as the threshold for a real change, not as typical session-to-session scatter. That note's conclusion (use several scans plus scale, waist and strength) stands.
- **Internal inconsistencies in sources.**
  - Antonio 2019: the abstract gives %BF change −1.3 ± 0.9 (DXA) and −1.4 ± 1.8 (InBody); its table gives −1.1 ± 0.9 and −1.5 ± 1.7. Table values are used here.
  - Potter 2022: the abstract says InBody over-predicted FFM by 2.7 kg; one table prints the all-subject FFM bias as −2.7. The abstract's sign matches the per-sex columns and the text, and is used here.
  - Velasquez 2026: the abstract gives a between-day BF% ICC of 0.965-0.998; the Results give 0.980-0.998.
