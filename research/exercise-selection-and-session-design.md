# Exercise Selection and Single-Session Design for Resistance Training

Last updated: 2026-10-21 (KQ5 addendum: short warm-up ramp)

Research notes compiled 2026-10-21. Scope: which exercises to include, how to cover every major muscle, how to order/structure a session, and how to substitute movements, so a program designer can fill 3- to 6-day templates. Note on evidence quality: many "best exercise" claims rest on single small trials (n = 14-63, often untrained or women-only samples) or on EMG, which repeatedly fails to predict hypertrophy (see Plotkin 2023 and the incline/flat pec data below). Treat exercise-specific effect sizes as low-to-moderate confidence unless stated otherwise. Access note: WebFetch and curl to strongerbyscience.com and pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov were blocked in this environment (DNS/proxy 403), so a few primary texts were read only through abstracts, repository records, or reputable secondary reviews; this is flagged per item.

## Key Question 1: Movement-pattern taxonomy, muscle coverage, and commonly undertrained muscles

### Takeaway
A session built from squat, hinge, horizontal push/pull, vertical push/pull, single-leg, carry and core patterns covers most large muscles, but the published hypertrophy and EMG literature shows that compound-only programs leave the medial/posterior deltoid, biarticular hamstrings, gastrocnemius, biceps, and long head of the triceps under-stimulated relative to isolation work performed at long muscle lengths.

### Cited Findings
- Back squat training (9 weeks, volume-matched vs hip thrust, untrained) produced greater quadriceps (+3.6 +/- 1.5 cm2) and adductor (+2.5 +/- 0.7 cm2) growth than hip thrusts, with similar gluteal growth between the two, i.e., the squat pattern covers quads, adductors and glutes; the hip thrust covers glutes but not quads — [Plotkin et al. 2023, Frontiers in Physiology](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2023.1279170/full)
- Deep (about 140 deg knee flexion) vs half squat (about 90 deg), 10 weeks, 17 men: gluteus maximus volume +6.7% vs +2.2% and adductors +6.2% vs +2.7%, with similar quadriceps and hamstring growth — [Kubo 2019 as summarized by Stronger By Science](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/squat-depth/); secondary confirmation at [Barbell Medicine](https://www.barbellmedicine.com/?p=250628)
- Hamstrings: in the squat/hip-thrust trial the hamstrings were not a primary beneficiary; the hamstring hypertrophy evidence comes from hinge and curl work (see KQ2). Prone leg curl eccentric biceps femoris EMG (93.3 +/- 37.7 mV) was significantly lower than Romanian deadlift (305.1 +/- 165.3 mV) in 12 men — [McAllister et al. 2014](https://www.unboundmedicine.com/medline/citation/24149748/Muscle_activation_during_various_hamstring_exercises_)
- Deltoids: barbell overhead press is reported (via a 2002 Behren and Buskies study cited secondhand) to elicit about 41% MVC in the anterior deltoid with lower lateral-head activation; frontal-plane or scapular-plane raises bias the lateral delt — [BarBend review of overhead press for hypertrophy](https://barbend.com/is-the-overhead-press-worth-it-for-hypertrophy)
- A 2020 EMG study found shoulder press and lateral raise both produced higher anterior and medial deltoid activation than bench press and dumbbell fly, i.e., horizontal pressing does not meaningfully train the side delt — [2020 EMG study, PMC7706677](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7706677)
- Posterior deltoid: reverse pec deck produced much higher posterior deltoid activity than incline lat pulldown or seated row in one EMG study; seated rear lateral raise and 45-degree incline row did not differ and both beat other tested exercises in a 16-lifter thesis — [Barbell Medicine, best rear delt exercises](https://www.barbellmedicine.com/blog/best-rear-delt-exercises/); [Sweeney thesis, UW Minds](https://minds.wisc.edu/items/ce6971e9-b01f-4c23-818c-4b432be66b11)
- Lateral raises with internal humeral rotation shift activation toward the posterior deltoid, upper trapezius and triceps; external rotation increases anterior and medial deltoid activation; frontal raises mainly activate anterior deltoid and pectoralis major (10 competitive bodybuilders) — [Coratella et al. 2020, IJERPH](https://ngdc.cncb.ac.cn:443/openlb/publication/OLB-PM-32824894)
- Triceps long head: 12 weeks of elbow extension with the arm overhead produced substantially greater triceps growth (about +19.9% volume) than with the arm by the trunk (about +13.9%), with the long head growing most, implying pressing-only or pushdown-only programs underload the long head — [Maeo et al. 2022 as summarized by sci-sport](https://sci-sport.com/en/triceps-hypertrophy-impact-of-arm-position/); [BarBend coverage](https://barbend.com/overhead-triceps-extensions-vs-pushdowns-muscle-growth-study)
- Gastrocnemius: standing (knee-extended) calf raise produced significantly greater volume gains than seated in lateral (12.4% vs 1.7%) and medial gastrocnemius (9.2% vs 0.6%) over 12 weeks in 14 untrained adults — [Kinoshita, Maeo et al. 2023, Frontiers in Physiology](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fphys.2023.1272106/full)
- Back/lats: wide-grip pulldown and seated row produced the highest latissimus-to-biceps activation ratio; seated row produced the highest middle trapezius/rhomboid activity; differences among lat exercises were small — [Lehman et al. 2004, Dynamic Medicine](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/1476-5918-3-4)
- Upper chest: untrained men doing incline-only pressing gained significantly and substantially more upper-pec thickness than flat-only or mixed groups, with no between-group difference in mid-pec growth — [Chaves et al. via Stronger By Science review](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/?p=57858)

### Inferences
- Movement-pattern taxonomy and primary coverage (synthesized from the EMG/hypertrophy findings above; cells without a citation are standard anatomy):

| Pattern | Representative exercises | Prime movers covered | Muscles NOT reliably covered |
|---|---|---|---|
| Squat (knee-dominant bilateral) | Back/front squat, hack squat, leg press | Quadriceps, adductors, gluteus maximus (more with depth, Kubo 2019) | Hamstrings (biarticular), calves, side/rear delts |
| Hinge (hip-dominant) | Deadlift, RDL, good morning, hip thrust | Glutes, hamstrings (RDL high BF EMG, McAllister 2014), erectors | Quads (hip thrust/RDL), calves |
| Horizontal push | Bench press (flat/incline), push-up, dips, machine chest press | Pectoralis major (incline biases clavicular head, Chaves), anterior deltoid, triceps (lateral/medial heads) | Medial/posterior deltoid, triceps long head (Maeo 2022) |
| Horizontal pull | Barbell/DB/cable/chest-supported row | Lats, mid-traps/rhomboids (Lehman 2004), rear delts (incline row, Sweeney), biceps, forearms | Lower lats are biased more by vertical pulls; biceps at long length |
| Vertical push | Overhead press, DB shoulder press, landmine press | Anterior deltoid, upper traps, triceps; medial deltoid partly (BarBend) | Posterior deltoid, medial deltoid relative to raises |
| Vertical pull | Pull-up, chin-up, lat pulldown | Lats, teres major, biceps (chin-up more), lower traps | Mid-traps/rhomboids (row is better, Lehman 2004) |
| Lunge / single-leg | Split squat, walking lunge, step-up, single-leg RDL | Quads, glutes (incl. medius), adductors, hamstrings (SL-RDL) | Calves |
| Carry | Farmer's walk, suitcase carry | Grip/forearms, traps, obliques, erectors | None uniquely; low hypertrophy evidence base |
| Core | Plank, cable crunch, hanging leg raise, Pallof press, back extension | Rectus abdominis, obliques, erectors | n/a |

- Commonly undertrained by compound-only programs and what to add (each addition is justified by the cited finding in parentheses):

| Under-trained muscle | Why compounds miss it | Add (first choice) | Add (alternative) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Medial (side) deltoid | Presses are anterior-delt dominant; horizontal presses give low medial activation (PMC7706677; BarBend) | DB or cable lateral raise, slight external rotation (Coratella 2020) | Lean-away cable raise, machine lateral raise, upright row to chest height |
| Posterior (rear) deltoid | Rows hit it only partially; reverse pec deck far higher (Barbell Medicine) | Reverse pec deck / rear-delt fly | Seated rear lateral raise, 45-degree incline row, face pull |
| Hamstrings (biarticular heads) | Squats grow hamstrings little; curls at short length grow less (Maeo 2021, KQ2) | Seated leg curl (long length) | RDL / stiff-leg DL, Nordic curl (RDL matched Nordic for BFlh architecture) |
| Gastrocnemius / soleus | No standard compound loads plantarflexion | Standing calf raise with full stretch (Kinoshita 2023; Kassiano 2023) | Leg-press calf raise; seated calf raise for soleus |
| Biceps | Rows/chin-ups train them, but not at long lengths; regional growth differs by exercise (KQ2) | Incline DB curl (proximal) + preacher curl (distal) | Bayesian cable curl, chin-ups |
| Triceps long head | Pressing and pushdowns keep the shoulder neutral (Maeo 2022) | Overhead cable/DB extension | Skull crusher with arms angled back |
| Upper chest (clavicular pec) | Flat-only pressing left upper pec behind (Chaves via SBS) | 30-degree incline press | Low-to-high cable fly |
| Mid-traps/rhomboids | Vertical pulls and pressing do not load scapular retraction (Lehman 2004) | Seated/chest-supported row | Face pull, reverse fly |
| Adductors / glute max | Partial-depth squats (Kubo 2019) | Squat to full depth | Adductor machine, sumo deadlift, Cossack squat |

### Gaps
- No longitudinal hypertrophy study comparing lateral raise vs overhead press for medial deltoid growth was found; the undertrained-side-delt claim rests on EMG and expert consensus.
- No hypertrophy trial on carries or on rear-delt exercises (EMG only).
- No single source quantified "how much" compound-only programs undertrain each muscle; the table above is an inference from exercise-specific findings.

## Key Question 2: Evidence-based best exercises per muscle group (lengthened/long-muscle-length training, EMG vs hypertrophy comparisons)

### Takeaway
The most consistent finding across 2021-2025 trials is that exercises or ranges of motion that load a muscle at long lengths produce equal or greater hypertrophy than short-length versions (seated vs prone leg curl, overhead vs neutral-arm triceps, long-length calf partials, incline curl for proximal biceps, deep squat for glutes/adductors), whereas pooled meta-analytic evidence shows lengthened partials are roughly equivalent to, not clearly better than, full ROM.

### Cited Findings
- Seated vs prone leg curl, 20 adults, within-subject, 70% 1RM, 5 x 10, 2x/week for 12 weeks, MRI: hamstring volume increased more with seated training (about 14% vs 9%), with the difference applying exclusively to the biarticular hamstrings that were more lengthened in the seated position; damage protection was similar — [Maeo et al. 2021, PubMed 33009197](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33009197/); figures via [sci-sport summary](https://sci-sport.com/en/hypertrophy-and-hamstring-protection-seated-prone-leg-curl/)
- Overhead vs neutral-arm triceps extension, 12 weeks: overhead produced greater triceps growth (about 19.9% vs 13.9% volume), long head grew most; popular articles mislabel this as "overhead vs pushdown" — [sci-sport summary of Maeo et al. 2022](https://sci-sport.com/en/triceps-hypertrophy-impact-of-arm-position/); [Biolayne REPS review](https://biolayne.com/reps/issue-4/are-you-pressing-down-your-triceps-growth/)
- Direct overhead extension vs pushdown (Stasinaki et al. 2018) found no significant difference, with nominal differences leaning toward overhead extension; only the long head was measured and findings were mixed across thickness vs CSA — [BarBend coverage](https://barbend.com/overhead-triceps-extensions-vs-pushdowns-muscle-growth-study)
- Calf partials at long muscle length: 42 young women, 8 weeks, 3x/week on horizontal leg press; initial-portion (long-length) group gained about 15% medial gastrocnemius vs about 7% full ROM and about 3% final-portion (short length); lateral gastrocnemius growth in the long-length group was similar to full ROM and both exceeded short-length — [Kassiano et al. 2023, JSCR, PubMed 37015016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37015016/)
- Standing vs seated calf raise, 14 untrained adults, 12 weeks: standing produced greater lateral (12.4% vs 1.7%) and medial gastrocnemius (9.2% vs 0.6%) growth — [Kinoshita, Maeo et al. 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fphys.2023.1272106/full)
- Preacher vs incline curl, 63 young women, 8 weeks, 2x/week: incline curl produced greater proximal elbow-flexor thickness, preacher curl greater distal thickness; preacher curl gave larger strength gain on the preacher test (specificity) — [Int J Sports Med 2025, PubMed 39809454](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39809454/); a 2023 trial in 31 trained women leaned toward preacher curl for overall growth but differences were not significant — [BarBend](https://barbend.com/news/preacher-vs-incline-biceps-curls/); practitioner synthesis at [Menno Henselmans](https://mennohenselmans.com/the-new-science-of-how-to-maximize-biceps-growth/)
- Incline vs flat press: in untrained men the incline-only group had a significantly greater and quite substantial increase in upper chest thickness, no differences in mid-chest; later EMG and swelling data (Albarello) did not match the longitudinal results; 30-degree incline gave the same upper-pec activation as 45 degrees but more lower-pec activation (Lauver); this is the only longitudinal study on regional pec growth by press angle — [Stronger By Science](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/?p=57858)
- Hip thrust vs back squat, 9 weeks, untrained (HT n=18, SQ n=16), MRI: gluteal growth similar with small between-group differences and wide CIs; squat grew quads and adductors more; gluteal EMG was higher in the hip thrust yet did not predict growth; authors have product-related competing interests — [Plotkin et al. 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2023.1279170/full)
- Squat depth: full squat (about 140 deg) beat half squat for glute max (6.7% vs 2.2%) and adductor (6.2% vs 2.7%) growth, quads/hamstrings similar; full squats improved full, parallel and half squat 1RM more than parallel or half training (Pallares 2019, 53 trained men, velocity-based, 10 weeks); only about three squat-depth and two leg-press-depth hypertrophy studies exist — [Stronger By Science squat depth](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/squat-depth/); [Barbell Medicine](https://www.barbellmedicine.com/?p=250628); [Pallares 2019 PDF](https://centros.edu.xunta.gal/cifpfarixa/aulavirtual/pluginfile.php/84864/mod_resource/content/1/17461391.2019.1612952.pdf); narrative synthesis: deeper squats favored anterior thigh, adductor and glute growth but differences were small when volume was equated — [Brookbush Institute](https://brookbushinstitute.com/articles/deep-squats-good-or-bad)
- Lengthened partials meta-analysis (Wolf et al. 2023, IJSC): partial and full ROM about equally effective overall; long-length partials subgroup favored partials with SMD -0.28 [95% QI -0.81, 0.16], imprecise, k = 8 — [SportRxiv preprint](https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/download/485/1040/972); a 2024-25 critical review concludes claims of superiority of long-length partials may be premature and effects may be region-specific — [Sports Medicine and Health Science review](https://www.sciopen.com/local/article_pdf/10.1016/j.smhs.2025.03.001.pdf); a 2025 within-subject trial in 30 trained adults (8 weeks) found similar thickness gains for full ROM and lengthened partials — [Biolayne REPS issue 34](https://biolayne.com/reps/issue-34/again-lengthened-partials-appear-comparable-to-full-rom-training/)
- Hamstring exercise: RDL vs Nordic (6 weeks, eccentric-biased) produced the same architectural changes in biceps femoris long head — [Physio Network review](https://www.physio-network.com/review/hamstrings-muscle-architecture-and-morphology-following-6-weeks-of-an-eccentrically-biased-romanian-deadlift-or-nordic-hamstring-exercise-intervention); RDL eccentric BF EMG far exceeded prone leg curl — [McAllister 2014](https://www.unboundmedicine.com/medline/citation/24149748/Muscle_activation_during_various_hamstring_exercises_)
- Leg extension hip angle: a 40-degree hip angle (more reclined) favored rectus femoris hypertrophy with no clear vastus lateralis difference (preprint) — [Baz-Valle et al. SportRxiv 407](https://doi.org/10.51224/SRXIV.407)
- Lats: no head-to-head pulldown vs row hypertrophy trial found; a registered within-subject trial (NCT07360236) is comparing lat pulldown vs row for lat and forearm growth — [ClinicalTrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07360236); EMG: wide pulldown and seated row best lat-to-biceps ratio, seated row best mid-trap — [Lehman 2004](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/1476-5918-3-4)
- Rear delt EMG: reverse pec deck much higher than incline pulldown or seated row — [Barbell Medicine](https://www.barbellmedicine.com/blog/best-rear-delt-exercises/)

### Inferences
- Muscle -> top exercises -> substitutions, with confidence rating (High = multiple RCTs/meta-analytic; Moderate = one RCT with MRI/US; Low = EMG or expert consensus only):

| Muscle | Top evidence-based choice(s) | Key study / effect | Confidence | Substitutions (barbell -> DB -> machine/cable -> bodyweight) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Quadriceps | Deep squat (full depth); leg press or hack squat; leg extension (reclined back for rectus femoris) | Kubo 2019: deep vs half similar quad growth but more glute/adductor; Plotkin 2023: squat +3.6 cm2 quad vs HT | Moderate | Back/front squat -> goblet or DB front squat -> hack squat, leg press, leg extension -> sissy squat, split squat, wall sit |
| Gluteus maximus | Deep squat or hip thrust (equivalent), deadlift | Plotkin 2023: similar glute growth; Kubo 2019: deep squat glute +6.7% vs +2.2% | Moderate | Barbell hip thrust -> DB/single-leg hip thrust -> glute drive machine, cable pull-through -> glute bridge, step-up |
| Hamstrings | Seated leg curl (long length); RDL/stiff-leg deadlift; Nordic curl | Maeo 2021: seated +14% vs prone +9%; RDL matched Nordic for BFlh architecture | Moderate | Barbell RDL -> DB RDL / single-leg RDL -> seated leg curl, 45-degree back extension -> Nordic/slider leg curl |
| Adductors | Deep squat, sumo deadlift | Kubo 2019 adductors +6.2% vs +2.7% | Moderate | Sumo DL -> Cossack squat -> adductor machine -> wide-stance bodyweight squat |
| Calves | Standing calf raise with full dorsiflexion stretch; long-length partials acceptable | Kinoshita 2023: standing 12.4% vs seated 1.7% LG; Kassiano 2023: long-length partial 15% vs full 7% MG | Moderate | Standing BB calf raise -> single-leg DB calf raise -> leg-press/standing machine -> single-leg raise on a step (slow, full stretch) |
| Pectoralis major | Flat press for mid pec; 30-degree incline for upper pec; include both | Chaves via SBS: incline-only grew upper pec substantially more | Moderate (single trial) | Barbell bench -> DB bench/incline DB -> machine press, cable fly -> push-up, deficit/ring push-up, dips |
| Anterior deltoid | Overhead press, incline press | About 41% MVC anterior delt in BB press (secondhand EMG) | Low | Barbell OHP -> DB shoulder press -> machine press, landmine press -> pike push-up, handstand push-up |
| Medial deltoid | Lateral raise (DB/cable), slight external rotation | Coratella 2020 EMG; no hypertrophy RCT vs press | Low | Upright row (to chest) -> DB lateral raise -> cable/machine lateral raise -> band lateral raise, side-lying raise |
| Posterior deltoid | Reverse pec deck; rear-delt fly; face pull; 45-degree incline row | Barbell Medicine EMG synthesis; Sweeney thesis | Low | Wide-grip BB row -> bent-over DB rear fly -> reverse pec deck, cable face pull -> band pull-apart, prone Y/T raise |
| Latissimus dorsi | Pull-up/chin-up; lat pulldown; row | Lehman 2004 EMG; hypertrophy trial pending | Low | Barbell row -> DB row -> lat pulldown, cable row -> pull-up, inverted row |
| Mid-traps/rhomboids | Seated/chest-supported row | Lehman 2004: highest mid-trap EMG in seated row | Low | Pendlay row -> chest-supported DB row -> cable row, reverse fly -> inverted row |
| Biceps | Incline DB curl (proximal) + preacher curl (distal) | IJSM 2025: regional split; 2023: preacher trend | Moderate | Barbell curl -> incline/preacher DB curl -> cable/Bayesian curl, preacher machine -> chin-up, inverted row underhand |
| Triceps | Overhead extension (long head); pushdown/close-grip press for lateral/medial heads | Maeo 2022: overhead about 19.9% vs 13.9% | Moderate | Close-grip bench/skull crusher -> DB overhead extension -> cable overhead extension, pushdown -> dips, diamond push-up |
| Erectors | Deadlift, back extension, good morning | Standard | Low | Deadlift -> DB RDL -> 45-degree back extension -> reverse hyper, bird dog |
| Abdominals/obliques | Cable crunch, hanging leg raise, Pallof press, carries | Standard | Low | Weighted sit-up -> DB side bend -> cable crunch, ab wheel -> plank, dead bug, hollow hold |

- Practical rule from the long-length literature: when choosing between two exercises for the same muscle, pick the one in which the target muscle is stretched under load (seated over prone curl, overhead over neutral triceps, incline over preacher for proximal biceps, standing over seated calf, deep over shallow squat). Expected advantage is modest (roughly 1.5x relative growth in the single trials above), and the Wolf 2023 meta-analysis suggests full ROM is at least as good as partials, so the default is full ROM with a hard stretch rather than deliberately truncated partials.

### Gaps
- No RCT found for lateral raise vs overhead press on medial deltoid hypertrophy, or for any rear-delt exercise on hypertrophy.
- No head-to-head hypertrophy data for pulldown vs row vs pull-up (trial registered, results not yet published).
- Stasinaki 2018 triceps and the 2023 preacher-vs-incline trial were only available via secondary coverage; exact effect sizes were not retrieved.
- Carry exercises have no hypertrophy literature found.

## Key Question 3: Exercise order: compounds first vs weak-point priority

### Takeaway
A meta-analysis of 11 studies shows exercise order significantly affects strength gains (exercises performed first improve most) but not hypertrophy, so the rule is: put the lift you most want to get stronger at, or the weak point you want to prioritize, first; otherwise order by fatigue cost (heavy compound -> light compound -> isolation).

### Cited Findings
- Nunes et al., European Journal of Sport Science 21(2):149-157 (2020/2021): systematic review and meta-analysis of 11 good-to-excellent quality studies (TESTEX); significant influence of exercise order on strength gains, with the specificity principle (exercise done first gains most) guiding programming; similar muscle hypertrophy regardless of exercise order — [Nunes et al. record](https://lida.sport-iat.de/ta/Record/4059795?lng=en)
- Secondary summaries claim the strength effect is larger for single-joint exercises placed first and cite 268 participants across 6-12 week programs, but these figures were not confirmed from the abstract — [FitChef summary](https://fitchef.com/studies/exercise-order-strength-hypertrophy/)
- Rest-pause/superset ordering interacts with fatigue: in agonist-antagonist paired sets, significantly more knee-extension repetitions were completed when preceded by knee flexion with no rest, 30 s or 1 min rest than with a traditional protocol — [Paz et al. 2014, PubMed 25148302](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25148302/)

### Inferences
- Order template for a session (derived from Nunes 2021 plus the volume/fatigue findings in KQ4 and KQ6):
  1. Priority or weak-point lift (the one lift whose strength or size you most want to move), fresh.
  2. Primary heavy compound for the day (squat/hinge/press/pull) if different from item 1.
  3. Secondary compound (opposite pattern or unilateral).
  4. Isolation work for undertrained muscles (side/rear delts, hamstrings curl, calves, arms), which tolerates fatigue and short rest.
- Because hypertrophy is order-insensitive, placing an isolation exercise first for a lagging muscle costs nothing for growth elsewhere and improves strength on that exercise.

### Gaps
- Full text of Nunes 2021 (moderator analyses for single- vs multi-joint and trained vs untrained) was not retrieved; the single-joint-first detail is from secondary sources only.

## Key Question 4: Rest intervals and tempo by goal

### Takeaway
For strength and hypertrophy with compound lifts, 2-3+ minutes between sets outperforms 1 minute (Schoenfeld 2016; Singer 2024 Bayesian meta-analysis trend); for isolation work 60-90 s is acceptable; repetition duration anywhere from 0.5 to 8 s produces similar hypertrophy, with very slow (10 s) reps inferior.

### Cited Findings
- Schoenfeld et al. 2016 (JSCR), 21 resistance-trained men, 8 weeks, 3 x 8-12 to failure on seven exercises, 1-min vs 3-min rest: 3-min group had significantly greater 1RM squat and bench gains and significantly greater anterior thigh thickness, with a trend toward greater triceps growth; thickness measured at one site only — [Brookbush Institute research review](https://brookbushinstitute.com/article/longer-interset-rest-periods-enhance-muscle-strength-hypertrophy-resistance-trained-men); viral "93% more growth" claims are not supported — [NASE blog](https://www.naseinc.com/blog/how-long-should-you-rest-between-sets-for-size-and-strength/)
- Grgic et al. 2017 (EJSS) systematic review: evidence equivocal, possible advantage for longer rest in trained participants, paucity of studies — [Grgic et al. 2017 PDF](https://researchgate.net/profile/Brad_Schoenfeld/publication/317832672_The_effects_of_short_versus_long_inter-set_rest_intervals_in_resistance_training_on_measures_of_muscle_hypertrophy_A_systematic_review/links/59a010ad0f7e9b18359db18f/The-effects-of-short-versus-long-inter-set-rest-intervals-in-resistance-training-on-measures-of-muscle-hypertrophy-A-systematic-review.pdf)
- Singer, Wolf, Schoenfeld et al. 2024 (Frontiers in Sports and Active Living) Bayesian meta-analysis of 19 measurements from 9 randomized studies; notes short rest (1 min) blunted myofibrillar protein synthesis vs 5 min (McKendry) and that short rest reduces volume load; a secondary source describes the pooled effect of longer rest as modest — [Singer et al. 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fspor.2024.1429789/full)
- Repetition duration: Schoenfeld, Ogborn, Krieger 2015 (Sports Medicine) meta-analysis of 8 studies (n = 239, 6-14 weeks, to failure): hypertrophy similar across 0.5-8 s per rep; very slow (10 s) training inferior — [PubMed 25601394](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25601394/); critique: included studies varied load and failure status, so results cannot be attributed to rep duration alone — [PeerJ 10909](https://peerj.com/articles/10909)
- Antagonist paired sets: 1-min rest between paired sets gave significantly greater training efficiency than 2- or 3-min; self-selected rest gave better performance, 2-min a balance — [paired-set rest interval study record](https://lida.sport-iat.de/ta/Record/4075858)

### Inferences
- Rest and tempo recommendations by goal (synthesis of the above):

| Goal / exercise type | Rest between straight sets | Tempo | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| Maximal strength, heavy compounds (1-5 reps) | 3-5 min | Controlled eccentric, explosive concentric | Schoenfeld 2016 strength advantage for 3 min; load maintenance |
| Hypertrophy, compound (6-12 reps) | 2-3 min | 2-3 s eccentric, 1 s concentric, pause at stretch | Schoenfeld 2016 thigh growth; Singer 2024; any 0.5-8 s rep is fine |
| Hypertrophy, isolation (10-20 reps) | 60-90 s | 2-3 s eccentric with brief stretch pause | Rest matters less when load/volume are easy to maintain; time-efficient |
| Antagonist paired sets | 60 s between the pair's exercises, 2 min between pairs | As above | Paz 2014; paired-set rest study |
| Muscular endurance / conditioning | 30-60 s | Continuous | Standard practice; limited hypertrophy evidence |

- Avoid prescribed 10-s reps; they reduce load and were inferior for hypertrophy in the 2015 meta-analysis.

### Gaps
- The exact pooled effect size and direction from Singer et al. 2024 was not retrieved (only its design and framing).
- A 2025 Enes/Schoenfeld tempo meta-analysis was referenced but its results were not found.

## Key Question 5: Warm-up protocols (general, ramp-up sets, static vs dynamic stretching)

### Takeaway
A brief general warm-up followed by exercise-specific ramp-up sets is best supported; static stretching held 60 s or more immediately before lifting reduces performance by about 4-5%, but short holds (<30-60 s) embedded in a full dynamic warm-up have negligible effect, so static stretching is optional and should be brief if used.

### Cited Findings
- Behm et al. 2016 (Applied Physiology, Nutrition, and Metabolism) systematic review: immediately after stretching, performance changed by about -3.7% (static), +1.3% (dynamic), -4.4% (PNF); static holds of 60 s or more per muscle produced larger deficits (-4.6%) than shorter holds (-1.1%); static stretching showed about +2.2% benefit at longer muscle lengths; stretching within a warm-up followed by dynamic activity is recommended for injury reduction and ROM with limited performance effects — [Behm et al. 2016 record](https://cir.nii.ac.jp/crid/1360011143567622912)
- Blazevich et al. 2016 (ECSS abstract, 20 athletes): 5-s and 30-s static stretches within a full sport-specific warm-up produced no group-level performance effect — [record](https://lida.sport-iat.de/ta/Record/4043874?lng=en)
- Specific warm-up for bench press and squat (40 trained men, 3 x 6 at 80% 1RM after each of three warm-ups: 2 sets at 40% and 80%; 6 sets at 80%; 6 sets at 40%): squat bar speed was higher after the 80% warm-up than after the 40% warm-up; prior work found about +3% upper-body power after specific bench warm-ups — [Ribeiro et al. 2020, PubMed 32971729](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32971729/)
- Systematic review of 11 studies on warm-up and strength performance: some studies showed no difference between specific warm-up and none, others found specific-warm-up-only was best for maximal strength; maximal strength and repetitions could be positively affected when the specific warm-up uses loads close to maximum — [Motricidade 2021 review](https://revistas.rcaap.pt/motricidade/article/view/21143)
- Heavier warm-up sets (potentiation) can boost subsequent performance per a REPS review of a trained-population study — [Biolayne REPS issue 29](https://biolayne.com/reps/issue-29/can-heavier-warm-up-sets-boost-your-training-performance/)
- Time-efficient training recommendations (Iversen et al. 2021, Sports Medicine, as cited by later papers): supersets targeting different muscle groups are preferred when time is the priority; the review also addressed warm-up and stretching — [Weakley et al. superset meta-analysis citing Iversen](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12011898/)

### Inferences
- Recommended warm-up template (synthesized): 3-5 min light cardio or dynamic movement (optional for well-trained lifters in a warm gym); dynamic mobility for the day's patterns; then ramp-up sets on the first compound only, e.g., empty bar x 10, 40% x 8, 60% x 5, 80% x 2-3, then working sets (pattern matches the 40%/80% two-set protocol of Ribeiro 2020). Later exercises need at most one feeder set at about 60-70%.
- If static stretching is used for ROM (e.g., ankles/hips for squat depth), keep holds under 30 s per muscle and follow with dynamic work (Behm 2016; Blazevich 2016).

### Gaps
- Iversen et al. 2021's exact warm-up/stretching recommendations were not read first-hand (full text inaccessible); they are known only through citing papers.
- No study found that isolated the effect of general (cardio) warm-up on lifting performance independent of specific warm-up.

### Addendum 2026-10-21: short ramp on the first compound

Why this addendum exists: the program replaced the four-step ramp in the Inferences above (empty bar x 10, 40% x 8, 60% x 5, 80% x 2-3) with a two-step ramp. The earlier findings and inferences stand as written. This section records the evidence behind the change and one correction to how Ribeiro 2020 has been summarised elsewhere in the repo.

Cited findings:
- Ribeiro et al. 2020, Int J Environ Res Public Health 17(18):6882 (peer-reviewed). Abstract re-read on PubMed in this session. 40 resistance-trained men, 19-30 years, did 3 x 6 at 80% of maximal dynamic strength (the "training load") after each of three warm-ups, 48 h apart: (i) WU, 2 sets of 6 at 40% and 80% of the training load; (ii) WU80, 6 reps at 80% of the training load; (iii) WU40, 6 reps at 40% of the training load. Results:
  - Squat: mean propulsive velocity was higher after **WU80** than WU40 (set 2: 0.69 vs 0.67 m/s, p = 0.02, ES = 0.80; set 3: 0.68 vs 0.66 m/s, p = 0.05, ES = 0.51). The abstract reports no squat difference for the two-set WU.
  - Bench: time to peak velocity was shorter after the **two-set WU** than WU40 (set 1: 575 vs 695 ms, p < 0.01, ES = 0.69; set 2: 533 vs 662 ms, p = 0.04, ES = 0.43), and total work was higher (4750 vs 4632 J, p = 0.01, ES = 0.54).
  - Authors' conclusion: force output was best after WU80 in the squat and after WU in the bench; "warming-up with few repetitions and low loads is not enough to optimize squat and bench press performances."
  - Outcome type: acute bar speed and power in the same session (strength-performance proxies). No hypertrophy outcome, no injury outcome.
  - Source: [PubMed 32971729](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32971729/); DOI 10.3390/ijerph17186882; PMC7558980.
  - Correction: the repo's earlier summaries (and the brief for this change) said the two-set warm-up beat six light sets on squat bar speed. The abstract says the squat difference was for the six-rep 80% warm-up; the two-set warm-up's advantage was on bench. Both readings support a near-working-load warm-up set over low-load volume.
- Motricidade 2021 systematic review (11 studies; peer-reviewed). Strength and reps benefited when the specific warm-up used loads close to the working weight; some studies found no difference between a specific warm-up and none. Not re-verified in this session: the journal site was down for maintenance. [Motricidade 2021](https://revistas.rcaap.pt/motricidade/article/view/21143)
- Heavier warm-up sets may potentiate the following working sets in trained lifters. Secondary source only: [Biolayne REPS issue 29](https://biolayne.com/reps/issue-29/can-heavier-warm-up-sets-boost-your-training-performance/).
- Injury prevention: ramp sets are commonly said to prevent injury. No study testing that claim was found in this note or in `research/recovery-nutrition-cardio.md` (Fradkin et al. 2010 on warm-up and injury was not retrieved).

Inferences (the program's new rule):
- Short ramp on the first compound: empty bar (45 lb / 20 kg) x 5, then 80% of the day's working load x 2-3. The structure follows the two-set WU of Ribeiro 2020 (one light set, one at 80% of the training load) with fewer reps: the bar replaces the 40% set and 2-3 reps replace 6 at 80% to save time and fatigue. The rep reduction is not tested in the study.
- When the top set is 225 lb (about 102 kg) or more, add 60% x 3 between the bar and the 80% set, because the jump from the bar to 80% gets large. **This step is coaching convention, not evidence.** No retrieved study tests it.
- Percentages are of the day's top working load (the V load on a volume day), not 1RM. Round to the nearest loadable step (5 lb with 2.5-lb plates; an exact half-step rounds up), never below the bar.
- Later main lifts keep one feeder set at 60-70%, unchanged.
- General cardio and dynamic mobility remain optional (see Gaps above). In a short session, cut them before the 80% set.
- Time: the four-step ramp took about 4-6 min; the short ramp takes about 2-3 min. The KQ6 time budget's "about 8 min warm-up" assumption becomes about 5-6 min.
- Why the old ramp fit poorly at current loads: with a 135-lb squat top set, 40% is 55 lb, barely above the 45-lb bar, so the set added time without adding a meaningful step.

## Key Question 6: Session design: exercises and sets per 45/60/90 minutes; supersets and paired sets

### Takeaway
Per-session hypertrophy returns plateau around about 11 fractional sets per muscle per session, while strength returns plateau at about 2 direct sets, and supersets/antagonist paired sets deliver the same repetitions and volume load in substantially less time (session-efficiency SMD = 1.74) at the cost of higher RPE and lactate.

### Cited Findings
- Weakley et al. 2024/2025 systematic review and meta-analysis (superset vs traditional): similar total repetitions (SMD = -0.03, p = 0.92) and volume load (SMD = 0.05, p = 0.86) with shorter session duration and increased training efficiency (SMD = 1.74, p = 0.01); higher blood lactate and RPE with supersets — [Weakley et al., PMC12011898](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12011898/); secondary: supersets reduced session duration without compromising volume, activation, perceived recovery, or chronic strength/endurance/hypertrophy, but induce higher internal load, more muscle damage and RPE — [MTN Tactical summary](https://mtntactical.com/research/research-review-supersets-vs-traditional-lifting-same-gains-less-time)
- A single trial found supersets completed sessions in 36% less time than traditional sets with similar strength and hypertrophy gains — [SportRxiv "Less time, same gains"](https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/view/419)
- Robbins et al. 2010 brief review: acute performance enhancement from agonist-antagonist paired sets is of limited practical applicability, but evidence suggests APS training is an efficacious and efficient means of developing strength and power — [Robbins et al. 2010](https://vuir.vu.edu.au/6830/)
- Paz et al. 2014: more knee-extension reps after knee flexion with no rest, 30 s or 1 min rest than in a traditional protocol — [PubMed 25148302](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25148302/)
- A 20-man study found the intensity used in APS may not increase target-muscle performance — [RBCM study](https://portalrevistas.ucb.br/index.php/rbcm/article/view/11024)
- Per-session volume meta-regression (Pelland et al., 67 studies, Bayesian): point of undetectable outcome superiority about 11 fractional sets per session for hypertrophy and about 2 direct sets per session for strength, with returns plateauing beyond — [Biolayne REPS issue 36](https://biolayne.com/reps/issue-36/too-much-muscle-math-finding-the-ideal-training-volume-per-session/); preprint — [SportRxiv 460](https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/view/460)
- Weekly volume/frequency meta-regression (Pelland et al., 67 studies, 2,058 participants): best-fit square-root curve, i.e., diminishing but gradual returns with increasing weekly sets — [Biolayne REPS issue 31](https://biolayne.com/reps/issue-31/the-king-of-volume-metas/)

### Inferences
- Time budget arithmetic (assumptions: a working set including setup takes about 45 s; rest per KQ4; about 8 min warm-up; about 3 min transitions): a straight-set compound with 2.5-min rest costs about 3.2 min per set; an isolation set with 75-s rest costs about 2 min; an antagonist paired set (two exercises, 60 s between, 2 min after the pair) costs about 4.5 min per pair, i.e., about 2.25 min per set.

| Session length | Straight sets only | With antagonist/unrelated-muscle supersets for accessories | Suggested structure |
|---|---|---|---|
| 45 min | 1 compound x 4 sets + 2 isolations x 3 sets (about 10 sets), 3 exercises | 1-2 compounds x 3-4 sets + 2-3 paired accessories x 3 sets (about 14-16 sets), 4-5 exercises | Priority compound straight sets, everything else paired |
| 60 min | 2 compounds x 3-4 sets + 2 isolations x 3 sets (about 14 sets), 4 exercises | 2 compounds + 3-4 paired accessories (about 18-20 sets), 5-6 exercises | Upper/lower or full-body day |
| 90 min | 3 compounds x 3-4 sets + 3 isolations x 3 sets (about 20 sets), 6 exercises | 3 compounds + 4-5 paired accessories (about 24-26 sets), 7-8 exercises | Body-part or push/pull/legs day |

- Per-muscle ceiling: do not program more than about 10-12 hard sets for one muscle in one session (Pelland per-session PUOS about 11); spread additional weekly volume across days instead, which is why 5- and 6-day splits exist.
- Superset rules: pair antagonists (chest/back, biceps/triceps, quads/hamstrings) or unrelated muscles (calves with lateral raises, abs with rear delts); keep the heaviest compound of the day as straight sets to protect load; expect higher RPE and accept slightly longer intra-pair rest for compound pairs (Weakley; Paz 2014).

### Gaps
- The exact per-exercise time figures are inferences; no study measured session duration for a defined 45/60/90-min template.
- Pelland per-session PUOS values come from a secondary review and a preprint; the peer-reviewed abstract was not retrieved.
- No long-term study isolates antagonist paired sets vs traditional sets for hypertrophy in trained lifters with matched volume beyond the trials pooled by Weakley.

## Key Question 7: Substitution rules (barbell -> dumbbell/machine/cable; bodyweight fallbacks)

### Takeaway
Free weights and machines produce equivalent hypertrophy (13-study meta-analysis), with strength gains specific to the tested modality, so substitutions should preserve the movement pattern, the loaded muscle length, and the rep/effort target rather than the implement; bodyweight fallbacks work when load is replaced with leverage, tempo, range, or proximity to failure.

### Cited Findings
- Haugen et al. 2023 (BMC Sports Science, Medicine and Rehabilitation), 13 studies, 1,016 adults, 6 trained / 7 untrained samples: free-weight training gave greater free-weight strength (SMD -0.210, CI -0.391 to -0.029, p = 0.023); machine training tended to give greater machine strength (SMD 0.291, CI -0.017 to 0.600, p = 0.064); no significant difference in hypertrophy or jump performance; authors conclude the modalities may complement each other — [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/a7282dba5e674176bde2a8239832d2f7); Stronger By Science notes the non-significant machine result may partly reflect fewer machine studies (6 vs 8) — [Stronger By Science](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/free-weights-vs-machines/)
- Strength is ROM/exercise specific: half squats improve half-squat 1RM most, full squats improve full-squat 1RM most (Kubo 2019), and full squats transferred to full, parallel and half 1RM better than partial training (Pallares 2019) — [Barbell Medicine](https://www.barbellmedicine.com/?p=250628)
- Preacher curl produced larger strength gains on the preacher test than incline curl (specificity) — [PubMed 39809454](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39809454/)
- Lat exercise variations produce only small changes in prime-mover EMG, so pulldown/row/pull-up variants are broadly interchangeable for lats — [Lehman 2004](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/1476-5918-3-4)
- Long-length principle generalizes across implements (seated vs prone curl, overhead vs neutral triceps, standing vs seated calf) — see KQ2 citations [Maeo 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33009197/); [Kinoshita 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fphys.2023.1272106/full)

### Inferences
- Substitution rules:
  1. Match the pattern (squat for squat, hinge for hinge, horizontal push for horizontal push).
  2. Match the muscle length where the exercise is hardest (choose the substitute that still stretches the target: e.g., replace seated leg curl with RDL, not with prone curl; replace overhead cable extension with DB overhead extension, not with pushdown).
  3. Match effort, not load: keep the same reps-in-reserve and rep range; expect absolute load to change (e.g., DB bench uses roughly 35-40% of the barbell load per hand; treat as a new baseline).
  4. For strength goals, keep the competition/primary lift itself at least once per week because strength is modality- and ROM-specific (Haugen 2023; Pallares 2019); substitutions are for accessories or travel weeks.
  5. For hypertrophy goals, substitute freely among barbell, DB, machine and cable (Haugen 2023 found no hypertrophy difference).

- Substitution matrix (bodyweight column gives travel fallbacks; progress bodyweight versions by adding range, tempo, pauses, unilateral loading, or reps to near failure):

| Barbell | Dumbbell | Machine / cable | Bodyweight / travel |
|---|---|---|---|
| Back squat | Goblet squat, DB front squat | Hack squat, pendulum squat, leg press | Bulgarian split squat, pistol/box pistol, sissy squat, 1.5-rep air squat |
| Front squat | DB front-rack squat | Hack squat (upright), leg extension | Heel-elevated narrow squat, wall sit + step-ups |
| Deadlift | Heavy DB RDL, trap-bar DL | Hip thrust machine, 45-degree back extension, cable pull-through | Single-leg RDL (backpack), Nordic curl, glute bridge march |
| RDL | DB RDL | Seated leg curl, back extension | Slider/towel leg curl, single-leg RDL |
| Hip thrust | DB hip thrust | Glute drive, cable kickback | Single-leg hip thrust (feet elevated) |
| Bench press | DB bench press | Machine chest press, cable press | Push-up (feet elevated, deficit, weighted backpack), ring push-up, dips |
| Incline bench | Incline DB press | Incline machine press, low-to-high cable fly | Pike/decline push-up, deficit push-up |
| Overhead press | DB/Arnold press | Machine shoulder press, landmine press | Pike push-up, handstand push-up, band press |
| Barbell row | Single-arm DB row, chest-supported DB row | Cable row, machine row, Hammer-strength row | Inverted row (table/rings), towel row |
| Pull-up | DB pullover (partial lat) | Lat pulldown, assisted pull-up machine | Door-frame pull-up, band-assisted pull-up, inverted row |

### Gaps
- No study was found that directly tests travel/bodyweight-only substitution periods on maintenance of strength or hypertrophy; the fallback column is a practitioner inference.
- Load-conversion ratios between implements (e.g., barbell to DB bench) were not sourced in this search.

## Key Question 8: Exercise variation: how often to rotate; variation vs consistency

### Takeaway
Planned, moderate variation (a small pool of exercises per muscle that hit different regions/lengths) matches or slightly improves regional hypertrophy and strength and raises motivation, while random or very frequent rotation and redundant exercises can hinder gains; keep core lifts fixed for a 4-12 week block and rotate accessories deliberately.

### Cited Findings
- Kassiano et al. 2022 (JSCR 36(6):1753-1762) systematic review, 8 studies, n = 241 young men, PEDro good/excellent: varying exercise selection can influence hypertrophy and strength; some systematic variation enhances regional hypertrophy and maximizes dynamic strength, whereas excessive random variation may compromise gains; redundant-stimulus exercises or very frequent changes may hinder adaptation — [review record](https://lida.sport-iat.de/ta/Record/4077216?lng=en); commentary notes the small evidence base — [Biolayne REPS issue 7](https://biolayne.com/reps/issue-7/exercise-variation-for-strength-and-hypertrophy-change-is-not-always-good/)
- Fonseca et al. 2014 (49 recreationally active men, volume-equated, MRI): groups that varied exercises showed hypertrophy in all four quadriceps heads while constant-exercise groups did not grow in some heads; overall CSA gains did not differ; title: "Changes in exercises are more effective than in loading schemes to improve muscle strength" — [Fonseca 2014 PDF](https://paulogentil.com/pdf/Changes%20in%20exercises%20are%20more%20effective%20than%20in%20loading%20schemes%20to%20improve%20muscle%20strength.pdf)
- Baz-Valle et al. 2019 (PLoS ONE 14(12)), 21 trained men, 8 weeks, 4x/week, 3 sets x 6 exercises, fixed vs app-randomized exercises each session: similar large bench and squat 1RM gains; vastus lateralis and rectus femoris grew similarly; vastus intermedius grew significantly only in the fixed group (no between-group difference); the varied group improved intrinsic motivation (moderate effect) while the fixed group declined non-significantly — [PMC6934277](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6934277); review at [Menno Henselmans](https://mennohenselmans.com/effects-exercise-variation-muscle-thickness-maximal-strength/)
- Strength gains are exercise- and ROM-specific (Nunes 2021 order effect; Kubo/Pallares depth specificity; preacher vs incline), which argues for keeping the lifts you test fixed — [Nunes et al.](https://lida.sport-iat.de/ta/Record/4059795?lng=en); [Barbell Medicine](https://www.barbellmedicine.com/?p=250628)
- Regional growth differences between exercises for the same muscle (preacher vs incline curl; incline vs flat press; seated vs prone curl) are the mechanism by which variation improves whole-muscle development — [PubMed 39809454](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39809454/); [Stronger By Science](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/?p=57858)

### Inferences
- Rotation policy for templates:
  - Primary strength lifts (squat, bench, deadlift, OHP, main row/pull-up): fixed across a 4-12 week block; change variants (e.g., pause squat, close-grip bench) only at block boundaries.
  - Hypertrophy compounds: 1-2 variants per pattern per week (e.g., flat and incline press; back squat and leg press), kept constant within a block.
  - Isolation/accessory: rotate every 4-8 weeks or when progress stalls; choose variants that change the region or muscle length (preacher <-> incline curl; seated curl <-> RDL), not redundant ones (Kassiano 2022).
  - Session-to-session random rotation is acceptable for motivation but gives no hypertrophy advantage and may blunt some regional growth (Baz-Valle 2019).

### Gaps
- No study defines an optimal rotation interval (weeks); the 4-12 week block guidance is an inference from block-length conventions and the specificity findings.
- All variation studies are in young men; no data for women or older adults.

## Key Question 9: Technique cues and common faults for the main lifts

### Takeaway
Coaching sources converge on a short list of faults per lift (hips shooting up and knee cave in the squat, loss of scapular retraction/leg drive and vertical bar path in the bench, back-lean and bar looping in the press, torso swing and lumbar rounding in the row), and recommend giving a lifter only one or two cues at a time.

### Cited Findings
- Squat: common faults are shifting weight to the heels (reduces quad involvement and depth; fix: tripod foot with pressure on big toe, little toe, heel), weak upper back causing forward lean (fix: squeeze shoulder blades), hips rising faster than shoulders (bar drifts forward; fix: keep knees forward early in ascent and maintain big-toe pressure); knees should track over toes without caving or bowing — [Breaking Muscle, 8 common squatting mistakes](https://breakingmuscle.com/8-common-squatting-mistakes-and-how-to-correct-them-for-stronger-squats/); [Livestrong squat mistakes](https://livestrong.com/article/13727691-squat-mistakes); "confident hips" cue for an S-shaped wobbling descent — [Juggernaut Training Systems, 5 common squat mistakes](https://help.jtsstrength.com/en/articles/57-5-common-squat-mistakes-and-how-to-fix-them)
- Bench press: the arch comes from scapular retraction and thoracic extension, not lumbar hyperextension; leg drive maintains the arch and shortens ROM, and its loss shows as sternum flattening and shoulders rolling forward; bar path is a slight inverted-J (toward the face then up), not a vertical line; common errors include skipping leg drive, not retracting the shoulder blades, pressing straight up, losing chest tension, bouncing the bar, and lifting the glutes off the bench — [FitnessVolt technique guide](https://fitnessvolt.com/powerlifting/guides/bench-press-technique-guide/); [PowerliftingTechnique leg drive](https://powerliftingtechnique.com/leg-drive-bench-press/); [PowerliftingTechnique 19 mistakes](https://powerliftingtechnique.com/bench-press-mistakes/); [Vitruve bar path](https://vitruve.fit/blog/the-correct-bar-path-in-bench-press/); Stronger By Science's bench guide covers foot position, grip width, touch-and-go vs pause, ascent bar path, leg drive, uneven lockout and butt rising — [Stronger By Science outline](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/?p=41732)
- Overhead press: the most common fault is leaning back too far; keep a tall chest (thoracic extension rather than a rigidly neutral T-spine); pull the chin back so the bar travels vertically past the face rather than looping forward; grip just outside shoulder width, braced core, stacked pelvis; choose one or two cues matching the weak point rather than all at once — [BarBend OHP mistakes](https://barbend.com/overhead-press-mistakes-fixes/); [Advanced Human Performance](https://www.advancedhumanperformance.com/upperbodytrainingmistakes); [Powerlifting Technique newsletter](https://newsletter.powerliftingtechnique.com/p/the-top-overhead-press-cues-for-strength-and-technique); [Breaking Muscle troubleshoot](https://breakingmuscle.com/troubleshoot-your-overhead-press/)
- Bent-over row (CSCS technique standard): hip hinge with torso at roughly 45-75 degrees, neutral spine, bar pulled to lower chest/upper abdomen, elbows driven past the torso, scapular squeeze at top; faults are torso swing and lumbar rounding — [CSCS resistance training technique guide](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/cscs/exercise-technique/resistance-training-technique)
- Hip hinge/RDL: Romanian deadlift form protects the back when the hinge is done correctly (hips back, neutral spine, bar close) — [Acibadem RDL form article](https://acibademinternational.com/blog/romanian-deadlift-form-the-hip-hinge-that-protects-your-back-when-done-right/)
- Squat depth for technique: full-depth squats are safe and superior for glute/adductor growth and transfer to all depths — [Stronger By Science squat depth](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/squat-depth/)

### Inferences
- Cue sheet (cited faults above plus standard coaching conventions; items marked * are standard practice without a retrieved citation):

| Lift | Setup cues | Execution cues | Common faults -> fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| Squat | Bar on traps (high) or rear delts (low); stance shoulder-width or wider, toes 15-30 deg out*; tripod foot | Big breath, brace 360 deg*; knees track over toes; sit between the hips; descend to full depth; drive floor away keeping chest up | Hips shoot up -> knees forward early, big-toe pressure; knee cave -> "spread the floor"*, lighter load; forward lean -> tighter upper back; heels only -> tripod foot; wobbling hips -> "confident hips" |
| Bench | Shoulder blades retracted and depressed; arch from T-spine; feet planted for leg drive; grip 1.5x shoulder width* | Bar to lower sternum, slight inverted-J path, forearms vertical at bottom*; drive legs into floor, press back toward face | Straight-up bar path -> cue J-path; shoulders roll forward -> re-pin scapulae; butt lifts -> lower arch/foot position; bouncing -> pause on chest |
| Deadlift | Bar over midfoot*; hips higher than knees, lower than shoulders*; lats engaged, slack pulled out*; neutral spine | Push the floor, keep bar in contact with legs, hips and shoulders rise together, finish with glutes not lumbar hyperextension* | Hips shoot up -> patience off floor, leg-drive cue; rounding -> brace, lighter load; bar drifting forward -> lats tight; jerking -> pull slack first |
| Overhead press | Grip just outside shoulders; forearms vertical; glutes and abs braced; stacked pelvis | Pull chin back, bar straight up past face, head through at lockout, tall chest (thoracic extension) | Excessive back-lean -> brace glutes/abs, cue tall chest; bar loops forward -> chin back; elbows flaring* -> forearms vertical |
| Row | Hinge 45-75 deg torso, neutral spine, bar below knees | Elbows drive past torso, pull to lower chest/upper abs, squeeze scapulae | Torso swing -> lighter load, chest-supported variant; lumbar rounding -> raise torso angle, brace |
| Pull-up | Shoulder-width or slightly wider grip, full hang with scapulae depressed* | Initiate by pulling shoulder blades down, drive elbows to ribs, chin over bar, control eccentric* | Kipping/half ROM* -> band assistance to full ROM; shrugging* -> depress scapulae first |
| Hip hinge / RDL | Soft knees, bar against thighs, lats tight | Push hips back until hamstring stretch, bar glides down thighs, spine neutral, return by driving hips forward | Squatting the RDL -> knees fixed, hips back; rounding -> stop at hamstring stretch; bar drifting away -> lats, bar on thighs |

- Stronger By Science's squat, bench and deadlift guides and Barbell Medicine technique articles could not be fetched (DNS/proxy blocked); only an outline of the SBS bench guide was retrieved via search.
- No pull-up technique source surfaced in search; the pull-up row is standard coaching practice and is marked with asterisks.
- No study was found linking any specific cue to injury risk or long-term performance; technique guidance here is coaching consensus, not trial evidence.
