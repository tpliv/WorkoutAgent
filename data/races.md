## Read (2026-11-08)

- **Faster by 44 s, all of it from pacing.** 2026 went out 23 s/mi faster
  over miles 1-5 and gave back 16 s/mi over miles 6-10. 2025 ran the first
  half conservatively and still closed in 8:12 and 7:28, so it left time on the
  course. An evenly paced 2025 would likely have been near today's time.
- **Higher cardiac cost.** Average HR 175 vs 169 (+6 bpm). At the stated
  10 miles that is about 1,533 beats per mile vs 1,493 (+2.7%). Highest HR seen
  in either race: 189. On that figure, 175 is about 93% and 169 about 89%. Both
  are normal for an 85-90 min race; 2026 was the harder effort for nearly the
  same speed. So the data show no gain in running fitness year on year, and
  maybe a small loss. Things that move race HR and are not fitness: heat and
  humidity, sleep, caffeine, illness, and bodyweight (the lifter was up 10+ lb
  in the 2-3 months before 21 Oct 2026; running cost scales with mass).
  Conditions and the 2025 bodyweight are not known.
- **Mile 9 (9:01) is the one real fade.** The last heavy lower session was the
  squat at 155 x 5 on Thu 5 Nov, R-3, after 19 days without a barbell squat.
  `guide/03` §4.7 puts the last heavy lower at R-4 or earlier, because new or
  unaccustomed eccentric work 24-72 h before a race slows running
  (`research/running-and-lifting.md` KQ2). Whether this caused the fade is not
  known. A stitch, fueling, or simply the faster start are equally possible.
  The lifter recovered to 8:39 and kicked at 6:56/mi, so it was not a blow-up.
- **Next year.** Even pacing at about 8:30-8:35 for miles 1-8, then race the
  last 2, is the obvious target on today's fitness (about 1:25:30-1:26). Beyond
  that the time has to come from fitness: more weekly running than the current
  under-10 miles, and heavy lower-body lifting plus a little plyometric work,
  which improves running economy by about 2-8% (`research/running-and-lifting.md`
  KQ3). Keep the last heavy squat at R-4 or earlier.
